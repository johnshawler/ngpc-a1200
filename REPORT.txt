Verdict:               FAIL. Stopped at milestone 10's gates after real attempts, at the user's decision (2026-11-14,
                       again 2026-11-15 after the display fixes and two rounds of logic work).
Game:                  Neo Turf Masters (SNK 1999, Neo Geo Pocket Color, World release, 2 MB cartridge, 60 Hz).
Class and route:       planar. Binary TLCS-900/H code: the Step 1 table has no row for this CPU; the route is the one of
                       "Assembly or disassembly for a Z80, 6502 or 6809 machine" (approved by the user in FEASIBILITY.md):
                       translated once on the host (tools/recomp900.py, entry points found by tracing on the reference
                       machine tools/ngpref) to C and to 68k assembly (tools/back900.py). Instructions with no inline
                       form are one C function each (gen_ops.c); nothing is interpreted at run time; code no trace
                       reached stops the game (ngp_fatal reason 2). K2GE, BIOS calls and interrupts are plain C
                       (src/ngpc/ngp.c). The Z80 sound program is not run: a model answers the game, and its music and
                       effects are recordings of the real program, mixed and played on Paula.
Display:               planes=6 scroll=1 fetch=1 buffers=3 sprites used=5 (sprite_w 64) colours=64 playfield + 16 sprite
                       bank. src/ngpc/hwdisp.c: the back scroll plane is a hardware-scrolled playfield of 16 x 16 tiles
                       (composed from the 8 x 8 cells, cached in chip RAM; rows in view drawn, buffer rows 256-415 repeat
                       0-159 for the vertical wrap; columns outside the 160-pixel window cleared to black); the front
                       plane and the sprites in front of it are five 64-pixel sprites in three strips (two 15-colour
                       attached pairs, one 3-colour strip on the right); small sprite objects are disp_bob. Only what
                       the game changed is drawn, within a time budget per picture (70% of its two frames, minus the
                       logic): the front layer first with half of it, the course with the rest, each resuming where the
                       last picture stopped. A new screen stays black until it is complete (at most 2 s). A new
                       picture every second vertical blank (PICTURE_HZ=25).
Busiest moment:        play (course scrolling, ball, shadow and cursor as objects, HUD digits and mini-map changing):
                       planned at 68.8% of a frame (FEASIBILITY.md section 5, logic 38% measured then). Measured over
                       the bench with 4 MB fast RAM: logic 20,603 colour clocks a tick (60 ticks a second: 2 or 3 ticks
                       a picture), worst tick 423,754 (scene setups); drawing 61,297 a picture, worst 284,454. The late
                       pictures hold on average 155,660 colour clocks of logic and 62,814 of drawing.
Cut from the original: 25 pictures a second instead of 60 (the brief's last cut, the user's decision; game time follows
                       the clock); the front plane and front sprites in 15 colours (3 in the right-hand 48 pixels: the
                       mini-map's water shows green), nearest colour for the rest (1.0-4.4% of pixels differ on the
                       host); large changes (a new screen, a front-plane scroll, the swing golfer) appear over several
                       pictures; the Z80 (music and effects recorded per command; stereo folded to one side per
                       voice; commands never recorded play nothing); saved games (flash writes forgotten); the serial
                       link; the date (fixed); raster effects (not modelled, not seen used).

Frame rate claim:      21.7 pictures shown a second (the 25-a-second mode), logic at 60 ticks a second (game time at 99%
                       of the clock), on the emulated A1200 with fast RAM. 25 is not held and 50 is not. Late pictures
                       are those with scene-setup logic (up to 423,754 colour clocks, 6 frames, in one tick) or 2-3 heavy
                       ticks. On the M1230 XA: not measured.
                       Cheapest upgrade likely to hold 25: a faster CPU; how fast the user's 33 MHz 68030 runs this code
                       is not known (see above).
Not done, not tested:  holding the gates: the logic needs to be roughly twice as fast, and several times in scene setups.
                       Peepholes gave about 5% (byte read-modify-write in one instruction); halving it needs a smarter
                       translator (pointers proven to stay in work RAM, the TLCS 8-bit register halves in 68k registers).
                       Real A1200 hardware and the user's 68030 (Kickstart 3.1 only in the emulator); NTSC; play by a
                       person (controls tested by scripts only); code no trace reached stops the game (976 functions are
                       translated); sound beyond the recorded commands; CI (the images cannot be committed to this
                       public repository).
How to run:            The floppy: tools/make_adf_ngpc.sh writes dist/ntm.adf (880 KB; it holds the cartridge: for the
                       owner only). Boot it on an A1200 with 4 MB (or more) fast RAM and Kickstart 3.x: it loads for
                       about a minute, unpacks for about a minute and starts the game.
                       From a hard disk: put the cartridge at original/ntm.ngc and, if you have it, the BIOS at
                       original/bios.ngp; tools/make_assets_ngpc.sh; tools/port.sh fast. Copy dist/ntm, dist/rom.bin,
                       dist/font.bin and dist/sound.bin to the Amiga and run "ntm" from a Shell ("ntm bench 3600" for
                       the proof run). Joystick in port 2: stick = pad, button 1 = NGPC A, button 2 = NGPC B. Keys:
                       cursor keys = pad, Z = A, X = B, Space or Return = Option, Esc = quit.
